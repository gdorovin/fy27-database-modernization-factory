# Renewal-led SQL playbook — charter

version: 1.0.0

For an engagement anchored to a licence or support-expiry date. The date is real, external,
and does not move, which changes what "good" looks like: a workload live before the date
beats a better architecture after it.

## Scope

SQL Server workloads inside the renewal or support boundary, and the evidence needed to
make a commitment decision before the date.

Deliberately out of scope until after the date:

- Estate-wide standardisation.
- Analytics and AI attachment.
- Anything that improves the outcome but cannot complete in time.

## Outcomes

1. **At least one workload is live under an approved cutover before the date.** A pilot
   that completes beats a programme that is still planning.
2. **Every remaining workload has a decision**, even where the decision is to retain with a
   review date. An undecided workload becomes an unbudgeted renewal line.
3. **The commitment is defensible.** The sponsor can say what was decided, on what
   evidence, and what was deliberately deferred.
4. **Nothing is rushed through the validation gate.** Time pressure changes the scope, not
   the standard of proof.

## Principles

Everything in the default playbook, plus:

**The date is a constraint, not a target.** Work backwards from it. A plan that needs
everything to go right has already failed.

**Deferring is a decision.** "We will look at it later" is not. Every deferral carries an
owner and a review date, so the second conversation starts from a position rather than from
nothing.

**Scope moves before the gate does.** When the date is at risk, the response is fewer
workloads in the wave. It is never a shorter validation or a waived blocking check.

**Retaining with a review date is a legitimate answer.** Especially here. A workload that
should not move before the date should be recorded as retained, not left ambiguous.

## Stakeholders

As the default, plus:

| Role | Responsibility | Holds decision rights over |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial lead | Owns the renewal negotiation and its timing | What the commitment must cover, and by when |

## Decision rights

As the default, with one addition:

- Deferring a workload past the date is the sponsor's decision, taken with the commercial
  lead, and recorded with a review date.
